# Layered Clothing Cages and Animation

<!-- tldr:start -->
> [!tldr] TL;DR

> - Avoid: Deleted or re-UV'd cage vertices -> Start from the cage templates; edit positions only
> - Avoid: Only an inner cage -> Both cages, outer adjusted
>

Layered clothing is Roblox's system for 3D garments that fit any body and stack over each other (a shirt under a jacket under a scarf). For an animator it matters because it changes what your clips look like on real players: clothes stretch, pinch and clip in extreme poses, long garments behave badly between the legs, and none of it is visible on the blocky test dummy. This note explains how the system deforms with animation, the specifications (Creator Hub, checked October 2026), and how to author clips and rigs that survive it.

## 1. The three layers of a layered accessory

| Layer | What it is | Role in animation |
|---|---|---|
| Mesh and textures | One MeshPart (SurfaceAppearance or TextureID) | What you see |
| Rigging armature | The garment is **skinned to the R15 armature** (weights saved in the mesh) | Makes sleeves follow shoulders and elbows when the character animates |
| Cages and attachments | An **inner cage** (fits onto whatever is under it) and an **outer cage** (what the next layer fits onto), plus at least one attachment | Makes it fit any body shape and stack with other garments |

Roblox docs: "Cage meshes set the inner and outer surfaces of a layered accessory." In Studio cages are represented by **`WrapLayer`** objects on garments and **`WrapTarget`** objects on bodies (both inherit `BaseWrap`, which holds `CageMeshId`, `CageOrigin`, `ImportOrigin` and their world-space read-only versions). Rigid accessories, by contrast, do not deform; they attach to a single point and move rigidly with one body part ([[Accessories and Armor That Follow Animation]]).

## 2. How it deforms when the character moves

Two separate things happen:

1. **Fit (wrap):** the garment's inner cage is matched to the outer cage of the body (or of the garment beneath), so the garment takes the wearer's shape. This is how one shirt fits a blocky body and an Rthro body.
2. **Skinning:** as joints rotate, the garment's vertices follow the armature by their skin weights, exactly like a skinned body ([[Weight Painting and Skinning for Roblox]]).

Skinning is what animation stresses. Weights come from one of two places:

| Source | How | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Authored | The artist weights the garment to the R15 armature in Blender or Maya | Full control; required for garments that should not simply copy the body (a stiff coat hem) |
| Automatic skinning transfer | `WrapLayer.AutoSkin` | Generates deformation from the cages at runtime |

`WrapLayer.AutoSkin` values (Roblox docs):

| Value | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| `Disabled` (default) | No transfer; an unskinned mesh will **not** deform with the character |
| `EnabledPreserve` | Generates skinning only if the mesh has none; keeps authored weights |
| `EnabledOverride` | Replaces all weights with the cage-based transfer |

The transfer only runs when layered clothing is enabled for the experience (`StarterPlayer.LoadCharacterLayeredClothing` Enabled or Default).

`WrapLayer.Order` sets the stacking order of garments; the reference page lists it without a description (commonly understood as lower numbers closer to the body). `BindOffset` and the reference mesh properties are plugin-level and used by the fitting tools.

## 3. Specifications that affect rigging

| Item | Value (Roblox docs) |
|---|---|
| Mesh | one mesh, at most **4,000 triangles**, watertight, quads preferred |
| Size | clothing within 8 x 8 x 8 studs around the attachment point; eyebrows and eyelashes 1.5 x 0.5 x 0.5 |
| Skinning | weighted and bound to an **R15 armature**; at most 4 influences per vertex; or generated by automatic skinning transfer |
| Cages | `<Name>_InnerCage` and `<Name>_OuterCage`, both required; outer identical to inner by default and only the outer is adjusted; never delete cage vertices or change cage UVs |
| Bodies | need an outer cage (`<Part>_OuterCage`) to accept layered clothing |
| Attachments | at least one, named by accessory type (for example tops use `BodyFrontAttachment`, bottoms `WaistCenterAttachment`; the Accessory Fitting Tool generates them) |
| Textures | up to 2048 x 2048 for the Marketplace |
| Importer | "Uses Cage" converts `_InnerCage` / `_OuterCage` meshes into WrapLayer / WrapTarget |

Cages work by **vertex correspondence**: the system matches vertices between cages by index and UV. That is why deleting cage vertices or editing cage UVs breaks fitting. Use Roblox's cage templates or Avatar Setup.

## 4. What this means for animation

### Poses that break clothes

Every garment is a linear-blend skinned mesh on top of another skinned mesh. The extremes that hurt bodies hurt clothes more:

| Pose | Typical artefact |
|---|---|
| Arms overhead | Shoulders of jackets stretch; armpit collapses |
| Deep crouch, splits, high kicks | Trousers and skirts stretch between the legs, waistband pulls down |
| Big wrist twist | Cuffs candy-wrap |
| Strong torso twist | Front panels shear; buttons slide |
| Arms crossing the chest | Sleeves interpenetrate the torso layer |

Rules of thumb for player-facing clips (players wear whatever they like):

- Prefer **arm raises under about 150 degrees** and keep elbows slightly out in overhead poses.
- Avoid holding full splits or deep lunges; pass through them.
- Keep the reference project's arm QA mindset: it flagged arms inside the body and blades through the body on a blocky dummy and noted that **slimmer avatars clear more easily than the QA said**. With layered clothing the opposite holds: puffy jackets and dresses clip **more** than the dummy predicts. Test clips on a "worst case" outfit.

### Long garments

Skirts, robes and long coats skinned to the legs stretch across the gap when legs separate. Options:

- `AutoSkin` with cage-based transfer, which handles this better than naive leg weights for many garments.
- Weight the hem more to the hips (`LowerTorso`) and less to the legs, accepting leg clipping through the cloth in big strides.
- For your own NPCs and creatures (not avatar items): use **dangle bones** (a cape or skirt chain like `Cape1 > Cape2 > Cape3` on the torso) driven by secondary motion in code, as the reference project's creature rigs did. See [[Idle Breathing and Secondary Motion]].

### Custom (non-R15) rigs

The documentation describes layered clothing in terms of the R15 armature and character bodies. Do not plan on layered clothing for creatures or non-R15 skeletons; use rigid accessories or modelled outfits instead. The reference project's shopkeeper NPC was a default R15 body plus a **modelled outfit** welded on, rather than layered garments.

### Cost

Wrapping and skinning are computed by the engine for every garment on every visible character. On screens with many characters (crowds, lobbies), rigid accessories and baked outfits are cheaper (rule of thumb). Measure with the MicroProfiler ([[Performance and Memory]]).

## 5. Weighting garments in Blender so they animate well

When you author the weights yourself (rather than relying on `AutoSkin`), a few practices carry over from body skinning and a few are garment-specific:

- **Start from the body.** Transfer weights from the body mesh to the garment (Data Transfer modifier, vertex groups by nearest face interpolated), then fix by hand. The garment then bends where the body bends, which is what the wrap expects.
- **Stiffen what should be stiff.** Collars, belts, armour-like panels and coat hems look wrong when they bend like skin. Push their weights toward a single bone (the upper torso for a collar, the lower torso for a belt) so they move rigidly with it.
- **Loosen what hangs.** Long sleeves and trouser legs can take slightly wider falloffs than the body at elbows and knees, so the fabric folds instead of pinching.
- **Same limits as bodies:** 4 influences, normalised, no root influence, no zero-weight vertices ([[Weight Painting and Skinning for Roblox]]).
- **Range-of-motion test with the garment on**, in Blender first and then in Studio: arms overhead, arms crossed, deep crouch, a stride, a torso twist. Fix the worst offender, then retest the others; garment fixes often trade one pose for another.
- Keep the garment's own geometry evenly dense at joints: a sleeve with two loops at the elbow will crease badly whatever the weights say.

## 6. Interaction with armour and rigid add-ons

Welded rigid pieces (armour, gear, weapon holsters) sit **over** layered clothing and ignore it. The reference project's armour system welded scaled plates to body parts and, for helmets, hid head accessories (hats, hair, face) by setting their transparency and storing the original values, rather than trying to fit over them. That is the pragmatic answer for gameplay gear. See [[Accessories and Armor That Follow Animation]].

## 7. Testing

1. Build a test dummy with a heavy outfit through a `HumanoidDescription` (jacket, long skirt or robe, scarf), or import garments and fit them with the Accessory Fitting Tool.
2. Play your locomotion set and every attack clip on it, in Play mode (wrapping and skinning are runtime).
3. Use `WrapLayer.DebugMode` / `WrapTarget.DebugMode` in Studio (not scriptable) to visualise cages when fitting looks wrong.
4. Note clip-specific issues in the clip's review ([[Animation Review Checklist]]).

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Result | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Unskinned garment with `AutoSkin` Disabled | Garment frozen at bind pose while the body animates | Skin it, or EnabledPreserve |
| Deleted or re-UV'd cage vertices | Garment fits wrongly or fails validation | Start from the cage templates; edit positions only |
| Only an inner cage | Next layer cannot fit over it | Both cages, outer adjusted |
| Body without an outer cage | Layered clothing does not equip | Add `<Part>_OuterCage` |
| Testing clips only on the blocky dummy | Clipping and stretching found by players | Test on a heavy outfit |
| Expecting layered clothing on creature rigs | Nothing fits | Rigid accessories, modelled outfits, dangle bones |
