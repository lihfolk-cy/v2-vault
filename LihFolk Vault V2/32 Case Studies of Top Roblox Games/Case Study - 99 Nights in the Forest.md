99 Nights in the Forest is a co-op survival game with a horror mascot. A small group lands in a dark forest, keeps a campfire burning, chops wood, crafts gear, fights wolves and cultists, rescues four lost children, and tries to survive 99 nights while a giant deer-headed creature hunts anyone who strays from the light. It reached about 14 million concurrent players, the third game in Roblox history to pass that level, with a team of five people. Of the three breakout hits of 2025 covered in this section, it is the one built most like a traditional game, and it has held its audience far better than the idle hits.

How to read the labels:
- **Public stats site**: Roblox's public game API, RoMonitor Stats, RoWatcher, Profitable.app.
- **Studio statement**: the developers, mainly a Roblox DevForum Creator Spotlight interview.
- **Press**: news coverage with outlet and date.
- **Observed in the live game (October 2026)**: the public store page, title, icon and thumbnails. Nothing here comes from playing or logging in.

Roblox does not publish CTR, retention or revenue for other people's games. Click-through reasoning below is inference.

## 2. Click-through: title, icon and thumbnails

### What is visible today (Observed, October 2026)

- **Title:** `99 Nights in the Forest 🔦`. No bracket tag, no update number, one emoji at the end (a flashlight, the item that stuns the monster). Trackers have recorded the studio using a front tag for limited boosts at other times, for example `[💎x2] 99 Nights in the Forest 🔦` for a double diamonds weekend (RoWatcher listing, date not shown).
- **Icon:** a close-up of the deer creature's face: huge round white eyes, open mouth, brown fur, on black. It sits inside the red "The Hollow" frame of Roblox's 2026 Halloween event, in which the Deer is the Day 1 monster (games.gg, October 22, 2026).
- **Thumbnails:** 4 images. The first two show the same scene, one framed for The Hollow and one plain: an avatar asleep with "Zzz" next to a campfire while the deer looms over them from the darkness. No text on the image.
- **Description:** three lines. "Build a camp with friends. Something is watching you. 99 Nights in the Forest".

### Why these choices plausibly earn clicks

1. **A mascot face.** The Deer is designed to be read at icon size: two white circles and a dark open mouth are visible even at 50 pixels. The studio said they wanted it scary but not off-putting to younger players and based its open-mouthed expression on an old screenshot (Studio statement, October 31, 2025). It is closer to a funny-scary cartoon than to gore, which keeps it safe for the home page.
2. **A story in one frame.** Fire, sleeping player, monster behind them. The viewer fills in "and then what?" The image needs no text because the situation is the hook.
3. **A number in the title.** "99 Nights" is a goal and a challenge. The studio chose 99 because it had a better ring to it than 100 (Studio statement). It also borrowed the "survive a number of days" trend popular on Roblox in early 2025 (Studio statement).
4. **Restraint as a brand.** In a front page full of bracket tags and rainbow numbers, a dark, wordless thumbnail with one creature stands out by contrast. The title does not change much; the brand is the monster.
5. **Platform event placement.** Being Day 1 of The Hollow puts the mascot in front of everyone joining Roblox's Halloween event, and the icon frame ties the game to that event.

## 3. First five minutes

From guides (rblxguide.com beginner guide, May 20, 2026; Item Level Gaming, July 1, 2025) and the store description. We could not confirm the exact lobby flow from a primary source.

1. You join a lobby (the root place) and go into a run with others; the run itself is a separate server in the forest.
2. You land at a small camp with a campfire and a crafting bench. You start with an axe and a small sack that holds only a few items (5, per the May 2026 guide).
3. The first task is obvious: chop nearby trees for logs and feed the fire. A guide recommends about 6 logs to survive the first night.
4. You open chests near camp, find food, maybe find a flashlight in a watchtower, and craft your first items at the bench (for example a map for 3 wood).
5. Night falls. The light shrinks to the fire's radius, you hear heavy footsteps, and the Deer appears at the edge of the light. A flashlight beam stuns it; the campfire keeps it away.

The first night arrives within the first few minutes and is the first "reward": a scary, memorable moment shared with friends. The tutorial is the environment. You learn the fire matters because it is the only light, and you learn the flashlight matters because the monster flinches from it.

See [[First-Time User Experience and Onboarding]], [[Lighting Mood and Time of Day]] and [[Teleporting and Multi-Place Games]].

## 4. Core loop and retention

### The run loop

- **Day:** go out from camp, chop, loot chests, gather food and scrap, kill animals for materials, explore further as the map grows.
- **Night:** return to the fire, defend against the Deer, wolves and (from about day 3) cultists, keep the fire fed.
- **Upgrade:** feed resources into the campfire (bigger safe radius) and the crafting bench (better recipes, storage, weapons, beds that shorten nights). Campfire levels gate the rescues (Item Level Gaming, July 2025).
- **Rescue:** four lost children are held in caves guarded by wolves or bears and opened with keys dropped by special enemies. Each rescue raises the run's progress rate. Reaching night 99 requires all four (Wikipedia list; Item Level Gaming).

### Meta loop

- **Diamonds** are the persistent currency, earned from chests, badges and the Cultist Stronghold, a repeatable dungeon the studio built partly as a diamond grind (Studio statement, October 2025; guides).
- **Classes** are bought with diamonds from a rotating daily class shop and upgraded over time. They give each run a different role (medic, ranger, lumberjack, and so on), which pushes co-op: a group wants a mix.
- **Badges** reward milestones and feed diamonds, so achievements are also currency.

### Live ops

- **Weekly updates** with a 45-minute "Update Party" sandbox session before each one, where the developers play with the community, hand out items and find bugs (Studio statement, October 2025).
- **Themed events**: a three-part Halloween update in 2025 with trick-or-treating by lighting houses at night, a candy currency, an event class, potions and limited items. The studio said players grind for events more than they expected (Studio statement, October 2025).
- **Biome and system updates** such as fishing and seasonal mini-biomes (guide sites, 2025 to July 2026).
- **Platform events and crossovers**: Day 1 monster in Roblox's 2026 Halloween event; a Fortnite collaboration announced August 2026 (Press).

### Why it holds better than the idle hits

Reasoning from the public numbers plus the design:
1. **Each run is a story.** Survival runs have a beginning, a rising difficulty and an ending. Players come back to beat their record, not to check on a timer.
2. **Co-op with roles.** Friends need each other, so groups return together. Social obligation is one of the strongest retention forces on Roblox ([[Social Design and Multiplayer Play]]).
3. **Content scales with the systems.** The studio described building systems and then pushing content "into the thousands" on top of them (Studio statement). Each update adds enemies, items and biomes to a game that already has depth.
4. **A mascot that can travel.** A film deal, a Fortnite crossover and a platform-wide event slot all depend on a memorable character, not a mechanic.

### The shape of the model

Light compared with the idle hits: a cosmetic-leaning pass (decorating the camp), with earlier class passes now off sale, and a premium currency that also drops in play. Classes are the main spending target, and they are also earnable through diamonds from badges and the Stronghold. Because a run is co-operative, buying power helps your team rather than hurting other players, which avoids the backlash the stealing games attract.

## 6. What to copy and what not to copy

### Copy

1. **A mascot readable at icon size.** Big simple shapes, two high-contrast eyes, one expression. Design the monster for the thumbnail first and the game second. [[Thumbnails Icons and Store Page]].
2. **The first night in the first five minutes.** Put the scariest, most memorable moment early and make it survivable. Teach through light and sound, not text. [[First-Time User Experience and Onboarding]], [[Lighting Mood and Time of Day]].
3. **A safe zone that grows.** The campfire radius is both a defence and a progress bar the player can see. Make upgrades change the world physically. [[Progression and Pacing Design]].
4. **A monster with a counter.** The Deer is stopped by a flashlight beam and by firelight. A threat that players can learn to handle feels fair and creates skill. [[Horror Monster Chase AI End to End]], [[Behaviour Trees Utility AI and State Machines for NPCs]].
5. **Roles bought with an earnable currency.** Classes give variety and a spending target without selling an advantage over other players. [[Currency Architecture - Soft Hard and Event Currencies]].
6. **Weekly update parties.** A short live session with the developers before each update builds community and catches bugs. [[Patch Notes and Player Communication]], [[Community Building]].
7. **Scope small, ship fast.** The game began as a one-week jam and shipped in about three months with five people (Studio statement). Prototype the hook, then grow systems that can absorb lots of content. [[Scoping and Roles for Small Teams]].
8. **Client-side responsiveness with server checks.** The programmer said they push as much as possible to the client so actions feel instant, with the server verifying and able to reject (Studio statement). Copy the idea, but keep currency, loot and damage authoritative on the server. [[Server Authority and Validation]].

### Do not copy

1. **Long runs without save points if your audience is young and mobile.** A survival run can last a long time; if a phone disconnects, the loss hurts. Plan for reconnects or checkpoints. [[Server Shutdowns Soft Updates and Migration]].
2. **A real horror tone for a young audience.** The studio deliberately kept the Deer funny-scary and debated making it more horrifying. Your rating and thumbnails must fit your audience. [[Roblox Policies Ratings and Compliance]].
3. **A trend name without a game under it.** Hundreds of "99 Nights" clones exist on Roblox (search results on RoWatcher). The trend name brought attention; the systems kept players.
4. **Unbounded content without performance budgets.** The builder said he keeps part, vertex and instance counts low for mobile (Studio statement). If you scale content into the thousands, enforce budgets. [[Mesh Optimization for Mobile]].

### A small build sketch: campfire safe radius

```lua
--!strict
-- Server-side: the fire's level sets a safe radius; monsters will not enter it.
local RADIUS_BY_LEVEL = { 20, 28, 36, 46, 58, 72 } -- studs, illustrative values

local function safeRadius(fire: Model): number
	local level = math.clamp(fire:GetAttribute("Level") :: number? or 1, 1, #RADIUS_BY_LEVEL)
	local fuel = fire:GetAttribute("Fuel") :: number? or 0
	if fuel <= 0 then return 0 end -- an unfed fire protects nobody
	return RADIUS_BY_LEVEL[level]
end

local function isInsideLight(fire: Model, position: Vector3): boolean
	return (position - fire:GetPivot().Position).Magnitude <= safeRadius(fire)
end

return { safeRadius = safeRadius, isInsideLight = isInsideLight }
```

The monster's AI checks `isInsideLight` before chasing a target, and the client draws the radius with the fire's light range so the rule is visible. Players learn the rule by seeing the creature stop at the edge.
