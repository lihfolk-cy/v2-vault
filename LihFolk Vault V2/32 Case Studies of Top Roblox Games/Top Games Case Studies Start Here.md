# Top Games Case Studies Start Here

This section tears down the most played and most clicked experiences on Roblox so you can borrow their lessons, not their games. Each note looks at one game from the outside: what its store page shows, how a new player's first five minutes go, what keeps people coming back, how it makes money, and which parts are worth building yourself.

## How to read these notes

- **Every number has a source and a date.** Most snapshots were taken from Roblox's public game API on October 27, 2026. Peaks and history come from press coverage and public stats sites such as RoMonitor Stats and RoWatcher. When a number could not be verified, the note says so instead of guessing.
- **Source labels** tell you how much to trust a line: "Observed in the live game (October 2026)" or "Observed on the store page" (the public page, icon, thumbnails and pass list), "Public stats site", "Studio statement", "Press", and sometimes "Community wiki" (lowest confidence). Nothing was played or logged into.
- **Roblox does not publish click-through rate, retention or revenue for other creators' games.** Anything about why a thumbnail earns clicks is reasoning from what is visible, not measured data. Your own game's numbers are in your Creator Dashboard; use [[A-B Testing and Experiments on Roblox]] to test your own art.
- **The snapshot week was unusual.** Roblox's Halloween platform event, The Hollow, was running, so most icons and titles were in seasonal dress. Treat the art descriptions as one frame of a moving picture.
- **Read for mechanisms, not themes.** Copying a theme puts you in a crowd of clones. Copying a mechanism (a shared restock timer, a visible risk window, a mascot readable at icon size) gives you a lesson you can put in any theme.

Every game note follows the same six parts: Snapshot, Click-through, First five minutes, Core loop and retention, Monetization, and What to copy and what not to copy, ending with a checklist. The "copy" sections link to the vault notes that teach each system, mostly in [[Genre Systems Start Here]], [[Economy Design Start Here]], [[Live Ops Deep Dive Start Here]], [[Worked Builds Start Here]] and [[Marketing and Growth Start Here]].

## Suggested reading paths

- **Making a simulator or idle game:** Grow a Garden, then Steal a Brainrot, then Pet Simulator 99. Pair with [[Genre Playbook - Simulators]], [[Simulator and RNG Genre Teardown]] and [[Rarity Ladders and Drop Pacing Math]].
- **Making a horror or survival game:** Doors, then 99 Nights in the Forest. Pair with [[Genre Playbook - Horror and Story]], [[Genre Playbook - Survival and Crafting]] and [[Horror Monster Chase AI End to End]].
- **Making a social or roleplay game:** Brookhaven RP, Adopt Me, Dress to Impress. Pair with [[Genre Playbook - Roleplay and Social Hangouts]] and [[Social Design and Multiplayer Play]].
- **Making a long-term grind or collection game:** Blox Fruits, then Fisch. Pair with [[Progression and Pacing Design]] and [[RNG Drops and Luck Systems]].
- **Working on your store page:** start with the cross-game note, then [[Thumbnails Icons and Store Page]] and [[Discovery and the Algorithm]].

## Before you start your own teardown

Use [[Competitor Analysis Method]] to study a game that is not covered here. Record the date of every number, separate what you observed from what you inferred, and check the game's store page again a week later: titles, icons and thumbnails change with every update, and the change itself tells you what the studio is testing.
