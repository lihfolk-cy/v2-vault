# Experience Description and Search Keywords

Search is the one discovery surface where the player tells you what they want. On Roblox it is semantic, natural-language search, so the old keyword-stuffing tactics do not work and the docs ask for accurate metadata ([[Discovery and the Algorithm]]). Since July 2025 search has a second value: a player who finds you through search can be attributed to you for [[Creator Rewards]]' Audience Expansion. This note covers how to write a title and description that match the queries real players type, how to test that, localisation for search, and the attribution angle. The general title and description template is in [[Thumbnails Icons and Store Page]]; this note does not repeat it.

Source labels: **Roblox docs**, **Rule of thumb**, **Unverified**.

## How search reads your page (what is known)

- Search is semantic and natural-language in officially supported languages (Roblox docs, discovery page). It matches meaning, so "game where you raise dragons" can match a description that says "hatch, feed and evolve dragons" without the exact word "raise".
- Accurate, relevant title and description are what the docs ask for. Stuffing genre words ("SIMULATOR RNG TYCOON OBBY") does not help and makes the page worse for humans.
- Roblox does not publish the full list of fields used for ranking in search. Genre settings and localised metadata are reasonable inputs to fill in carefully; treat any claim about exact weighting as Unverified.

## Write for the query, not the algorithm

Start from what players would type. Collect 15 to 30 candidate queries (rule of thumb):

- The genre in plain words ("tower defense", "obby", "horror escape").
- The fantasy ("be a dragon", "run a pizza shop", "escape the school").
- The verb ("fish", "hatch pets", "build a base").
- The theme or setting ("pirates", "anime", "space").
- Things a parent or friend might type ("game to play with friends", "easy obby").

Then make sure the **first two lines** of your description say, in natural sentences, the top three or four of those ideas. Example of the structure, not a template to copy:

> Hatch, feed and evolve baby dragons, then race them across floating islands with friends.
> NEW: Storm Isles - lightning dragons and a sky boss.

The first line combines verb, subject, fantasy and social hook. The second line is the freshness hook that changes every update.

Rules of thumb:

- **Title**: the brand plus at most one descriptive phrase. The title is the strongest single signal of what the game is; keep it accurate and short.
- **First 150 characters** do the work; many surfaces truncate the description.
- **Synonyms in sentences, not lists.** "Raise and evolve" in one sentence, "train your dragon" in another.
- **Names players search for.** If your game is known by a nickname or abbreviation, use it once in the description.
- **No competitor names** or trending game names you are not related to. It misleads, and it is the kind of manipulation the docs warn against.

## Test your search position

You cannot see Roblox's search logs for your game beyond what the dashboard provides, so test manually:

1. Use a fresh account or logged-out browser view where available (personalisation affects results).
2. Search each of your candidate queries; record your position or "not in top results".
3. Change the description's first lines to address the queries where you are absent; wait for re-indexing (the docs give no indexing delay; rule of thumb is to check again after a few days).
4. Keep a dated sheet. Change one thing per round.

Watch the acquisition breakdown in your dashboard for search-sourced plays; that is the outcome that matters, not your position for one query.

## Localise for search

Semantic search works per supported language, so a Portuguese, Spanish or Indonesian player searching in their language is matched against your metadata in that language. Localised title and description for your top non-English regions is one of the cheapest discovery wins available (Rule of thumb; see [[Localization]] for the workflow). Translate the meaning and local genre words, not word-for-word: genre names differ by community.

## Search attribution and Creator Rewards

Creator Rewards' **Audience Expansion** pays a share of a new or reactivated user's qualifying purchases in their first 60 days when that user is attributed to you through a share link, a direct link **or a search for the experience**, plays 10+ minutes on the join day, and your experience averages 100+ DAU over the following 60 days (Roblox docs, Creator Rewards). Practical consequences:

- Your **brand name being searchable** matters. When a video or a friend mentions your game, viewers often search for it by name; make sure the exact title and common misspellings or nicknames resolve to you.
- **Off-platform mentions should use your exact title**, so search finds it.
- Model this revenue in your unit economics ([[Unit Economics - ARPDAU, Payer Conversion, LTV and Ad Payback]]).

## Keeping it honest and current

- Update the "NEW" line every update; remove stale event text within a day of an event ending.
- Every claim must be true for every player who reads it ("50 worlds" must exist).
- Benefits of passes and subscriptions described accurately.
- No external social handles in the description or in-game text beyond what Roblox currently allows for eligible users ([[Community Building]]).

## Common mistakes

- **Keyword lists** in title or description.
- **Clever first lines** that do not say what you do ("A journey awaits...").
- **Using other games' names** to catch their searches.
- **English-only metadata** for a game whose players are mostly Brazilian or Indonesian.
- **Stale event text** weeks after the event.
- **Changing title, description and icon at once,** so you cannot tell which change helped.

## Checklist

- [ ] 15 to 30 candidate queries written from the player's point of view.
- [ ] First two description lines cover verb, subject, fantasy, social hook and the current update.
- [ ] Title is the brand plus one accurate descriptor.
- [ ] Localised title and description for top regions.
- [ ] Manual search position sheet, re-checked after changes.
- [ ] Brand name and nicknames resolve to your game in search.
- [ ] Description reviewed for truth after every update.
