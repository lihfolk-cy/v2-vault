# Marketing and Growth Start Here

This section is about getting players to a Roblox game and keeping the ones who arrive: launching, paid campaigns, short-form video, update rhythm, studying the market, and testing changes properly. It sits on top of the platform notes in `11 Monetization Publishing and Live Ops`, which explain how discovery, thumbnails, analytics and ads work. Read those for the mechanics; read these for the strategy and the maths.

One idea runs through every note here: **on Roblox, growth is mostly retention made visible.** The recommendation system ranks games by per-user averages from players who arrived organically through Recommended for You: play-through rate, early bounce, return days, capped playtime, co-play and spend days (Roblox docs, as of October 2026; see [[Discovery and the Algorithm]]). Paid and off-platform traffic does not count in those signals. Marketing can bring players to the door, but only the first session, the return hooks and honest promises turn that into lasting traffic.

## The honesty rule
No fake reviews, bot traffic, bought likes, misleading thumbnails, "free Robux" hooks, or titles borrowing another game's name. Each note explains why these break Roblox's Community Standards or thumbnail rules, but the practical reason is simpler: they move vanity numbers and damage the numbers that matter. A misleading thumbnail raises clicks and bounce together; bot visits do not count toward ranking and corrupt your own analytics; a broken promise in a teaser costs you the players most likely to return. See [[Ethical Design and Player Trust]].
