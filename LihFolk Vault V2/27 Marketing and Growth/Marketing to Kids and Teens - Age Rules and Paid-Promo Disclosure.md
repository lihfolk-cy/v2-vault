# Marketing to Kids and Teens - Age Rules and Paid-Promo Disclosure

A large share of Roblox players are children and teenagers, so almost every marketing decision for a Roblox game touches child-directed advertising rules: what you may say in the game, how paid creators must disclose, who may run ads, and what Roblox changed in 2026. This note collects the rules that apply to developers marketing their own game, with sources. It is not legal advice; for laws such as COPPA (US) or the UK Age Appropriate Design Code, consult a lawyer, especially before running paid influencer campaigns.

Source labels: **Roblox docs**, **Roblox announcement** (official DevForum post), **Reported** (press or watchdog report), **Rule of thumb**.

## What changed in 2026

- **Ads permitted to all users.** The Terms of Use update announced on 30 March 2026, effective 30 April 2026, says the changes "aim to improve ad transparency, clarify ad eligibility" and that ads will be permitted to all users, with clarification of who sees personalised versus non-personalised ads (Roblox announcement: https://devforum.roblox.com/t/updates-to-the-roblox-terms-of-use-april-2026/4548394). Previously, Roblox's Advertising Standards (effective June 2023, Reported) limited advertising to users under 13. In practice, assume younger users may see your ads and that **non-personalised** delivery applies to them; write creative accordingly.
- **External links gated by age.** Social links on experience pages are visible only to age-checked 16+ users since 30 June 2026, and external social handles in game text are no longer allowed (see [[Community Building]] and [[Age Checks and Safety Features]]).
- **Roblox Kids and Roblox Select audiences** (announced April 2026) change who can find games with certain content; see [[Policy and Age Rules 2026]].

## Who can buy ads

Ads Manager requires users to be 13 or older with a verified email; converting Robux to ad credits is available to users 13 and up; card payments are 18+ (Roblox docs, Ads Manager). The Engagement objective targets "age-checked highly engaged players" (Roblox docs). If a team member under 18 runs campaigns, someone over 18 handles card billing.

## Creative rules for a young audience (rule of thumb, informed by the above)

- **No false scarcity or pressure aimed at children**: countdown timers that reset, "only 3 left" when stock is infinite, "everyone is buying this".
- **No implied free Robux** or rewards you do not control. Roblox's terms prohibit promising Robux, and scams use this framing.
- **Show the real game.** Over-promising creative is both a policy risk and a funnel killer ([[Sponsored Ads and Campaign Math]]).
- **No collecting personal data** in marketing flows: no "enter your email/phone for a reward" in game or on landing pages targeting kids.
- **No external links in the game** to stores, socials or Discord except where Roblox allows them for eligible users.
- **Avoid using real children's likenesses** (player videos, faces) without verifiable consent from guardians.

## Paid creators and disclosure

When you pay or give anything of value (Robux, items, early access, exclusive cosmetics) to a creator for content about your game, it is a material connection that must be disclosed.

- Under US FTC endorsement guidance, disclosures must be clear and conspicuous; for child audiences, watchdogs expect language children understand.
- In 2023 the Children's Advertising Review Unit (CARU) found Roblox's then-program for influencers fell short of its guidelines for children; Roblox agreed to ask child-directed influencers to use the word **"paid"** in disclosures (Reported: BBB National Programs CARU decision, https://bbbprograms.org/media/newsroom/decisions/roblox-advertising). Roblox disputed parts of the finding.

A practical brief for creators you sponsor:

1. Say it out loud early in the video and in text on screen: "This video is paid for by [studio]" or "#ad / paid". Hashtags alone at the end of a description are not enough.
2. Use the video platform's own paid-promotion label as well.
3. Do not script claims that are false or unverifiable ("the best game on Roblox").
4. Do not ask creators to direct children to external sites, giveaways requiring personal data, or Robux "giveaways" contingent on joining a group.
5. Keep records of what you paid and the disclosure used.

See [[Working with Content Creators]] for campaign logistics.

## In-game promotion

- **Group join rewards and invites** are allowed but must not be spammed; prompts should be gated and capped ([[Community Building]]).
- **Do not reward likes, favourites or off-platform social actions** (Roblox discourages this; see the Community Building note for the details and sources).
- **Brand or sponsored content inside your game** (a real-world brand's item, a sponsored event) must be labelled as advertising and comply with Roblox's advertising standards; when in doubt, label it.

## Platform rules differ off Roblox

Video and social platforms have their own minimum ages (commonly 13), "made for kids" settings that disable comments and personalised ads, and their own branded-content rules. If your content is directed at children, mark it as such on the platform even though it reduces reach; mislabelling is a legal risk in some countries (Rule of thumb; check each platform's current policy).

## Common mistakes

- **Assuming under-13 users never see ads** after April 2026. Write creative that is appropriate for them.
- **Paying creators without a disclosure brief.**
- **"Free Robux" framing** in ads, thumbnails or creator content.
- **Collecting emails or phone numbers** from players for rewards.
- **External links in game text** after the 2026 changes.
- **Rewarding social actions** (likes, favourites, follows).

## Checklist

- [ ] Ad creative suitable for under-13 viewers and shows the real game.
- [ ] No false scarcity, no Robux promises, no personal data collection.
- [ ] Ads Manager access and billing set up by eligible ages.
- [ ] Written disclosure brief for every paid creator: spoken, on-screen, platform label, word "paid".
- [ ] In-game sponsored content labelled as advertising.
- [ ] Off-platform content marked "made for kids" where it is.
- [ ] Records of creator payments and disclosures kept.
